## Where They Part

### Jupiter

When this person says yes to something large (a plan, an offer of help, a venture someone brings them), the two charts give that yes opposite fates. In the Tropical chart Jupiter is in fall in Capricorn in the second house, ruled by a peregrine Saturn in the seventh, so the generosity gets counted after it's given. They back the plan, then sit with the figures, and the pleasure of having offered thins as the accounting comes in, with a promise made to one other person setting the limit on what they let themselves spend. In the Sidereal chart Jupiter is in its own sign in Sagittarius in the first house, forming Hamsa Yoga in Purva Ashadha, so the same yes lands on a body and a set of circumstances that hold up while they move toward it. A setback rinses off and the next attempt starts clean.

Both accounts describe the same act, and each comes with its own consequence. One leaves a debit to be carried and the other leaves ground that recovers. A wide square from Uranus jolts both, coming from friends and group plans in one frame and from the tenth in the other. From the inside, this person can finish a generous week feeling both depleted and renewed, with no way of telling which of the two will be waiting when they wake.

### Sun

What this person makes, and how they come to know it's theirs, splits cleanly between the frames. The Tropical Sun is peregrine in Taurus in the sixth, borrowing its terms from Venus in the seventh, so a self gets built through daily reliability and confirmed only once someone close has reflected it back in words. The Sidereal Sun is exalted in Aries in the fifth, in Bharani, ruling the ninth, so a self acts at full strength inside creation and play, needs no one to sign off on it, and answers personally for whatever it brings into being or ends.

The split shows in the moment something they've made is finished. In one account they hold it out and wait for the verdict, unsure of themselves until it comes. In the other they already stand behind it and are ready to take the blame if it fails. Both rulers sit in the seventh house, so even the exalted Sun's terms are set by Mars among the people they argue with. The overlap gives them no shared answer. What they live with is a self that is provisional in the work they repeat and decisive in the work they invent, and those two stretches of a week do not consult each other.

### Midheaven

The standard this person's working life is measured against differs between the frames. The Tropical Midheaven is in Libra, air and cardinal, while the tenth house stays Virgo, so the public aim tilts toward balance through relationship: being the one who keeps a working arrangement fair and the people in it on terms. The Sidereal Midheaven is in Virgo, earth and mutable, in the same sign as Pluto, so the aim becomes usefulness through precision: being right, exact and of service, under exacting pressure that sits directly on the vocation.

When their work is judged, one account asks whether everyone involved can still work together, and the other asks whether the thing is correct. They can give a fair result that fails on accuracy, or an exact one that leaves a colleague bruised, and feel each time that they've met the standard and missed it.

### Neptune

The blurred, unmeasured thing in this person's partnerships turns up in different places in each chart. Tropical Neptune is in Sagittarius in the first house, just past the start of the sign. It's ruled by Jupiter in fall and opposed by Saturn in the seventh, so faith belongs to how they enter, and the opposition sorts a relationship into two roles. They become the one who keeps the terms, and the other person becomes the one who drifts. Sidereal Neptune is in Scorpio in the twelfth, in Anuradha, ruled by a peregrine Mars in the seventh. The same opposition now runs from Saturn in Taurus in the sixth, so the vagueness is their own. It shows up as fatigue they can't see, an expense with no clear source, or loyalty spent out of sight, and it seeps into the daily routine.

When an agreement with a partner goes unclear, the Tropical account has them correcting the other person's imprecision. The Sidereal account has them working harder at their own schedule to cover a loss they haven't named. The Saturn sub-period running to February 2027 is putting this opposition forward now in both frames. They can spend a month fixing a partner's details and come out of it wondering why they themselves are so tired.

Saturn and Uranus also change sign and house between the frames, and they stay apart as well, two more places where the accounts don't meet.

On an ordinary evening this person has given generously, finished something, been judged at work and tidied an agreement, and none of it adds up to one verdict. The generosity stands as a cost and as a renewal, and the finished thing is both waiting for a reply and already owned. The work was fair and might not have been exact, and the tiredness belongs to a partner's vagueness or to their own. They tend to settle each question by whichever account the day's mood favours, and by the next morning they've often settled it the other way. That is the price of these four shifts: they rarely get to know, in the moment, what kind of day they've just had.

## Jupiter and Saturn

### Jupiter in Sagittarius, House 1

Your faith carries a sense that you've already won, and you act on that sense before anything has confirmed it. Jupiter sits in Purva Ashadha, a nakshatra ruled by Venus and presided over by Apas, the water goddesses, and its instinct is early, undefeated confidence that keeps clearing obstacles the way water keeps moving round a stone. In this frame Jupiter moves back one sign, from Capricorn in the second to Sagittarius in the first. The steady ground you walk on has already been described. What this nakshatra adds is the momentum. You commit to a direction as though the outcome were settled, and that conviction often carries you through a stretch where the evidence is thin.

The same confidence shows its other side once an early success arrives. You tend to treat the first win as proof of the whole road, and the next stage, which needs the humility you skipped, catches you underprepared and surprised that belief alone stopped working. Because Jupiter is retrograde, you usually recognise this afterwards and in private, as a deflation nobody else sees.

Jupiter's gaze onto your ninth house falls on an empty sign. Your sense of meaning gets no outside support from formal teaching, travel or doctrine, so you rely on your own conviction instead of an institution's. You tend to trust what you've concluded for yourself over anything a tradition hands you. When that conclusion is wrong, nobody is placed to correct it in time.

### Saturn in Taurus, House 6

Your endurance lives in the daily grind, and in this frame it's the thing that pays for everything Jupiter starts. Saturn moves back one sign, from Gemini in the seventh to Taurus in the sixth. That's a different situation from the agreements kept with a partner: it describes the obligations kept with your work and your body. Taurus is a friend's sign for Saturn, because Venus rules it and the two are natural allies, so the discipline here feels like a comfortable rhythm more than a punishment. You build skill by repetition and keep a routine long after others have dropped theirs.

Saturn rules your second and third houses, which carries money, effort and your own initiative into the sixth. Earnings come through labour and maintenance, and whatever you set out to do on your own gets turned into a job with hours attached. Rohini, ruled by the Moon and presided over by Brahma the creator, makes you attached to the work itself: its texture, its craft, the satisfaction of a thing made well. Under strain that attachment becomes possessiveness over your methods, and you resist any change to how you do the task even when the change would spare you effort.

Neptune casts its gaze onto Saturn from your twelfth, and that's what unsettles the routine. Vague tiredness, an illness without a clear cause, or work that dissolves into unpaid hours wears at the structure, and your response is to hold the schedule tighter. Saturn looks back at Neptune and down onto Ketu in your eighth, so you keep carrying other people's hidden burdens as though they were your shift too. The Saturn sub-period running to February 2027 puts all of this in the foreground now.

You live with your conviction in your body and your labour in the sixth house. You set out sure of the outcome, and then you meet the long middle, where only steady work moves anything. You recognise the pattern when a plan you were certain of turns into a schedule and a tiring body, and the belief that started it has gone silent while you keep turning up to finish it.

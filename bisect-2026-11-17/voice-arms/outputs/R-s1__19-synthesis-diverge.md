## Where They Part

Mars is where the two accounts separate furthest. In the Tropical chart Mars sits in Libra at 15°56' in the twelfth house, in detriment, and its own ruler is Venus in Scorpio, also in detriment. Neither link in that chain can act cleanly. The will that wants to press is asked by its sign to weigh fairness first, and the planet it answers to demands proof before it will move. The Tropical pattern is assertion held back by a question about whether pressing is just. In the Sidereal chart Mars sits in Virgo at 22°15', still in the twelfth, peregrine, in Hasta. Its ruler is Mercury in Libra in the first house, retrograde and peregrine. That chain carries less strain, and the problem it gives this person is different. The will is asked to correct and refine, and the planet it answers to turns a thought over again before releasing it. The Sidereal pattern is assertion held back by a question about whether the work is right yet.

The two accounts separate most clearly where the Mars-Saturn sextile engages, tight at 2.8° and applying, with Saturn in the second house in both frames. Something needs confronting inside an arrangement this person has already committed to, such as a role, a shared obligation or a standing agreement. Under the Tropical account, with Saturn in Sagittarius, they test the grievance against their conviction about what is fair. They bring it forward as a reasonable proposal and come away with the arrangement intact and the original want never stated. Under the Sidereal account, with Saturn in Scorpio ruled by this same Virgo Mars, they turn to the structure itself and repair it quietly until the fault no longer shows. They come away with work that is sound and a complaint nobody ever received. The private hour is the same in both accounts. The Tropical account spends it deciding whether they have the right to object. The Sidereal account spends it making the objection unnecessary.

The Ascendant divides at the threshold of every new situation. Tropical Scorpio rises at 6°57'. It is fixed water, ruled by that detriment Mars, and this person enters a situation by probing for what is concealed. Sidereal Libra rises at 13°16'. It is cardinal air, and its lord is Venus in Scorpio in the second house, in detriment and conjunct Saturn in a Raja Yoga. Under this account the same entrance is organised around whether the visible terms of exchange have balanced. The collision comes when this person meets a new partner or joins a new group. The Tropical account has them reading the undercurrent: who holds power, and what has been withheld. The Sidereal account has them keeping a ledger of who gave what and whether it evens out. Each account produces information the other has no use for. A hidden motive does not change a balance sheet, and a fair balance sheet does not clear a suspicion. So this person can leave a first meeting holding a settled account of the exchange alongside an unsettled verdict on the person, and neither answers the other.

The Sun shifts in sign and in dignity. Tropically it is Scorpio at 9°32', peregrine, in the first house, ruled by Mars. Sidereally it is Libra at 15°51', debilitated, in Swati, with Venus as its lord. The Sun-Pluto conjunction, at 0.4° and separating, survives both frames intact, and it is what sets this divergence off. A careless remark from someone close, or a shift in where their attention goes, lands as a threat to identity itself. Under the Tropical account, Scorpio's Sun takes that threat inward and holds it at full depth. This person withdraws to work out what the slight revealed, and they are left carrying a stake the moment never named aloud. Under the Sidereal account, the debilitated Libra Sun checks its own standing against the other person's before it will claim injury. It looks for the version in which both sides are partly right. That leaves this person holding a fair settlement while still unsure whether they were entitled to be hurt at all. The conjunction intensifies the remark under both accounts. The two accounts disagree about what intensity licenses.

Jupiter changes condition more than any other planet in the chart. In the Tropical chart it is Aries at 22°43' in the sixth house, peregrine and retrograde. Its ruler is the detriment Mars, so the urge to begin over in work and the body runs back through a will that defers. In the Sidereal chart it is Pisces at 29°02', still in the sixth, in its own sign, forming Harsha Yoga. That yoga confines difficulty to its own house and classically turns it to advantage there, though it promises no ease. Sidereally Jupiter sits within a degree of the sign's end, while Tropically it stands well clear of any boundary. The Jupiter-Uranus trine, tight at 1.6°, sets this one off: a routine breaks without warning. Under the Tropical account, with Uranus in the second house, the break registers as a chance to start fresh. This person launches a new regimen at once, and its value is then counted against how secure they feel. Under the Sidereal account, with Uranus in the third house, the same break registers in the daily mind. This person absorbs the disruption into the existing routine and adapts the routine around it. They come away with continuity preserved, which leaves them with no clear point at which anything changed. One account gives them a fresh start, and the other gives them a routine that has stretched to fit.

The two accounts also part across Ketu, the Midheaven, Rahu, the Moon, Neptune, Mercury, Pluto, Saturn, Venus and Uranus, each drawn differently by the two charts and none of them settled here.

The tension that runs through all four is where this person's restraint comes from. When they hold back, whether from a confrontation, a claim, a judgement of someone new or a fresh start, the Tropical chart traces the restraint to doubt about whether they have the right to act. The Sidereal chart traces the same restraint to a standard that has not yet been met. The behaviour looks the same from both sides, so this person can easily treat one cause as the explanation for both. The cost is that a solution which works on one cause leaves the other untouched. Granting themselves permission does not finish the correcting, and finishing the correction does not supply the permission. Both explanations fit the same hesitation, and each time it happens this person is left not knowing which one applies.

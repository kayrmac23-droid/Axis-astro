## The Sun

Swati's lord is Rahu, and its deity Vayu presides over wind: movement that bends without snapping, direction that keeps shifting because staying fixed would cost more than the shift does. What this adds to a Sun that the sign alone does not show is a need for your sense of self to stay unfixed, free to move when a room or a role closes in. You feel this as a restlessness underneath the Libra composure, a readiness to go elsewhere the moment belonging starts to feel like containment.

The sign has moved backward from Tropical Scorpio to Sidereal Libra, though the house has not: this Sun still sits in the first, so the self it describes is still the one you arrive with before you have done anything deliberate. But Libra's logic replaces Scorpio's. Where the Tropical Sun probed a room for what was concealed, this Sun measures a room for whether it has settled into balance, and whether your presence in it has become one of the fixed weights or stayed free to move. Debilitated in its own house, this Sun must work for a steadiness that a differently placed Sun would simply have.

The Lagna analysis has already named the Raja Yoga this Sun's dispositor sits inside: Venus, ruler of the Sun's sign, conjunct Saturn in the second house, kendra and trikona lords joined. What that yoga gives the self is not ease but structure earned the hard way, worth and stability tested through the same strict gate before either is granted. This Sun carries that same requirement into the first house directly: your vitality and self-expression are made to answer to a standard before they are permitted to simply exist, and the debilitation means that permission is slower arriving here than the yoga elsewhere might suggest.

The conjunctions with Mercury and Pluto, both also in Libra in the first house, mean retrograde thought and Plutonic intensity are not separate forces acting on this identity. They are folded into the same unit, so the self you present carries a mind constantly revising itself and a depth that does not let go, both running through a dignity already working at a deficit. House lordship adds a further strand: this Sun rules the eleventh, the house of community and the future, so whatever uncertain confidence it manages to produce gets carried into networks and long-range hope rather than staying private.

Mars, ruling the sign of the Sun's exaltation, sits in a kendra from the Moon, a classical condition that tempers the debilitation without erasing it: the fall is real, but it is not total collapse. The Mercury antardasha, just begun within the larger Ketu mahadasha, puts this exact configuration to work now: Mercury sits conjunct this very Sun, retrograde, in the first house, so the chapter ahead asks your identity to be revised and re-presented rather than settled, with Ketu's larger pull toward release still the mahadasha's governing condition underneath it. The pattern goes quiet wherever a role has already proved its fairness and no longer needs weighing; it is most active anywhere your standing has not yet been tested against that standard, which, under this dasha, is most rooms you currently enter.

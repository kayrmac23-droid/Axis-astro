## Where They Part

### Mars: Libra in the Twelfth, Virgo in the Twelfth

The Tropical Mars sits at 15°56' Libra in the twelfth house, in detriment, and it rules the Scorpio Ascendant. The will that runs this person's whole approach to life therefore works through a sign that asks it to weigh before it strikes. Its dispositor, Venus in Scorpio in the first, is in detriment too, so nothing in the ruler chain steadies it, and assertion turns into negotiation conducted out of view.

The Sidereal Mars sits at 22°15' Virgo, still in the twelfth, peregrine, in Hasta Pada 4. Its dispositor is Mercury in Libra in the first, peregrine and retrograde. Here the same withheld drive is read as craft: Hasta asks for the work of the hands, and the correction happens on the object itself.

The close sextile to Saturn, 2.8° and applying, is where the two accounts lead to different acts. Saturn sits in the second house in both frames, so the trigger is someone else breaking a standard or commitment that bears on what this person owns or earns. Under the Tropical account they open terms, adjust their claim toward what seems fair to both parties, and leave with a settlement that cost them part of the original demand. Under the Sidereal account they correct the structure itself: the figures redone, the agreement rewritten cleanly, the grievance never said aloud and still theirs to carry.

The Tropical Mars carries more pressure, compromised by sign and by dispositor at once. The Sidereal Mars has a workable tool, and a dispositor that the current Mercury antardasha brings into the foreground. The wide opposition to Jupiter adds a thin pull across the sixth in both frames.

### The Ascendant: Scorpio 6°57', Libra 13°16'

The Tropical Ascendant is Scorpio, fixed water, and it opens every new situation with a search for what is being withheld. The Sidereal Lagna is Libra, cardinal air, and it opens the same situation by measuring the terms between people and asking whether they have come to rest. The element shifts from water to air, so one account enters a room through depth of feeling and the other through proportion.

Each ruler is in detriment. The Tropical ruler is Mars, hidden in the twelfth. The Sidereal Lagna lord is Venus in Scorpio in the second, where the Raja Yoga with Saturn binds it to a slow test of value.

In a first meeting with a new group, the Tropical account has them reading for the concealed motive, and they leave holding a private verdict about who in the room can be trusted. The Sidereal account has them initiating and setting the terms of exchange, and they leave holding an arrangement whose worth Venus in the second has not yet let them believe. Both readings form within the same first hour. Neither ruler is in better condition, so the pressure falls evenly across the two.

### The Sun: Scorpio Peregrine, Libra in Fall

The Tropical Sun at 9°32' Scorpio in the first house is peregrine, and its dispositor is the same Mars in detriment in the twelfth. The tight conjunction with Pluto, 0.4° and separating, makes identity a matter of depth, with every stake registered at full weight.

The Sidereal Sun at 15°51' Libra in the first is debilitated. Its dispositor is Venus in detriment, and its nakshatra is Swati Pada 3, ruled by Rahu, with Vayu presiding. In this account the self is calibrated against other people and carries a mobility that will not stay fixed in one stance. The Sidereal account carries more pressure here: fall at the Sun itself, detriment at its ruler.

The conjunction with Pluto sets the scene off in both frames, with Pluto in Scorpio in one and in Libra in the other. The trigger is someone close shifting loyalty without saying so. Under the Tropical account this person goes straight to the motive and comes away with a judgement about the other person's trustworthiness that they are slow to revise. Under the Sidereal account they first test whether their own standing entitles them to object, and come away still unsure the grievance was theirs to raise.

### Jupiter: Aries Peregrine, Pisces Domicile

The Tropical Jupiter at 22°43' Aries in the sixth is peregrine and retrograde, ruled by Mars in detriment. Its appetite for starting the better routine therefore depends on a will that defers. The Sidereal Jupiter at 29°02' Pisces in the sixth sits in its own sign, retrograde, in Revati Pada 4, and forms Harsha Yoga: a difficulty-house lord in its own house, classically turning adversity in work and health toward advantage, with no promise of ease.

The Sidereal degree lies near a sign boundary and the Tropical one does not. Within its own frame the placement is fully Pisces. The Tropical account is under more pressure here, while the Sidereal Jupiter disposes itself.

The tight trine to Uranus, 1.6° and separating, triggers both accounts when something unexpected arrives. In the Tropical chart it comes through Uranus in the second, as a jolt to what counts as secure; in the Sidereal it comes through Uranus in the third, as new information in daily exchange. Under the Tropical account they launch an overhaul of their working routine and are left with a strong start that its dispositor has not licensed them to finish. Under the Sidereal account they fold the new demand into the existing routine and are left with a larger workload, one that the close trine to Saturn made feel reasonable at the moment they took it on.

The divergence continues, unwalked, across Rahu, Ketu, the Midheaven, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto, each keeping the two accounts apart.

In the Tropical chart one compromised planet, Mars in detriment in the twelfth, rules the Ascendant, the Sun and Jupiter. In the Sidereal chart Venus in detriment in the second rules the Lagna and the Sun, while Mars and Jupiter answer to peregrine Mercury and to Jupiter itself. The same life therefore routes its difficulty through two different gates: a will that gives its claims away out of sight, and a valuation that will not certify what it already holds. A single episode, a broken commitment or a quiet shift in loyalty, is fully explained by either gate, and each explanation names a different cost: a claim surrendered, or a holding never trusted. This person is likely to take whichever cost they noticed first as the whole account of what happened, while the other cost goes on accruing, unread.

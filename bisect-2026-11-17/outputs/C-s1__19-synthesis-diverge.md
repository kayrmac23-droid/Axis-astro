## Where They Part

Mars carries the widest gap between the two charts. It is the planet that rules the Tropical Ascendant, and it changes sign, dignity and dispositor at once. In the Tropical chart Mars sits in Libra at 15°56' in the twelfth house, in detriment, and its dispositor is Venus in Scorpio in the first, also in detriment. Here the will to act is asked to work through a sign built for agreement, and it answers to a ruler that will not move without proof. The result is assertion that negotiates with itself out of view. In the Sidereal chart Mars sits in Virgo at 22°15', still in the twelfth, peregrine, in Hasta Pada 4. Its dispositor is Mercury in Libra in the first, peregrine and retrograde. Read in Jyotish terms, Mars rules the second and seventh houses from a Libra Lagna and hides both in the twelfth, so resources and open partnership are settled through private, exacting labour.

The two accounts meet when the Mars–Saturn sextile, 2.8° and applying, is triggered by a commitment already made. This might be an obligation that has gone wrong or a sum owed on agreed terms. Under the Tropical account they weigh the grievance toward a settlement that keeps the exchange intact. What they are left with is an agreement they consented to and partly resent. Under the Sidereal account they correct the terms clause by clause until the complaint becomes unanswerable. What they are left with is an exact document and anger that was never spent on anyone. The Tropical Mars carries more pressure, since detriment rests on a detriment dispositor. The Sidereal Mars works from a neutral footing, though it is ruled by a retrograde planet that keeps reopening the draft.

### The Ascendant

The Tropical Ascendant falls at Scorpio 6°57', water and fixed, and is ruled by that same detriment Mars. The Sidereal Lagna falls at Libra 13°16', air and cardinal, and is ruled by Venus in Scorpio in the second, in detriment. Each frame therefore makes a different planet chart ruler, and each of those rulers is compromised in its own way. Consider a first meeting where something is being proposed. The Tropical account sends this person after the motive behind the proposal, and they leave knowing what was concealed but not whether to accept. The Sidereal account has them measuring whether the exchange is evenly weighted, and they leave with a fair-looking arrangement whose value Venus has not yet agreed to trust. The two frames ask different questions of the same proposal, and the chart gives neither answer the final word.

### The Sun

The Tropical Sun stands at Scorpio 9°32' in the first house, peregrine and ruled by detriment Mars. The Sidereal Sun stands at Libra 15°51' in the first, in fall, in Swati Pada 3, and is ruled by detriment Venus. The Sun–Pluto conjunction, at 0.4°, is one relationship, but each frame reads it through its own sign. Suppose someone makes a careless remark about this person's standing. Under the Tropical account the remark registers as an intrusion on identity itself. They answer at depth and are left having made the moment heavier than its cause. Under the Sidereal account the fallen Sun checks its own claim against everyone present before it will defend it. They stay level and leave carrying a self-assessment that waits on other people's verdict before it will close. In this pairing the Sidereal Sun is under the heavier strain. The cancellation of the debilitation that the Sidereal reading drew softens the fall without removing it. The Tropical Sun has no dignity to lose, and the pressure on it comes through Mars.

### Jupiter

The Tropical Jupiter sits in Aries at 22°43' in the sixth house, peregrine and retrograde, and is ruled by detriment Mars. Growth arrives here as the urge to begin again inside the routine. The Sidereal Jupiter sits in Pisces at 29°02' in the sixth, domicile and retrograde, in Revati Pada 4. It is its own dispositor, and it carries Harsha Yoga as lord of the sixth placed in the sixth. That late degree places the Sidereal Jupiter near the edge of Pisces, while the Tropical Jupiter sits well inside Aries. Each is fully in its sign within its own frame.

The Jupiter–Uranus trine, at 1.6° and separating, sets the scene when a routine breaks without warning. Under the Tropical account they treat the break as licence and launch the rebuilt regimen that week. They are left with a fresh start whose ruler, Mars, has not authorised it, so the follow-through stalls. Under the Sidereal account they absorb the disruption into the routine whole, letting the change spread through work and body without marking a starting point. They are left with a routine that has quietly widened, and no clear moment at which they decided to take the extra on. The Tropical Jupiter is the one under pressure, working from a peregrine sign through a detriment dispositor. The Sidereal Jupiter works unobstructed in its own sign, and its Harsha Yoga promises advantage through difficulty without promising ease.

The divergence continues, unwalked, across Ketu, the Midheaven, Rahu, the Moon's house, Neptune, Mercury's house, Pluto, Saturn, Venus's house and Uranus's house, each keeping the two accounts apart.

All four of these divergences run through the same chain of rulers. In the Tropical chart, Mars governs the Ascendant, the Sun and Jupiter, and it is in detriment. In the Sidereal chart, Venus governs the Lagna and the Sun while Jupiter governs itself, which leaves Mars governing only the confrontations it keeps out of sight. So when this person reaches a decision, it can be routed through two different rulers. A grievance is settled for the sake of peace in one account and corrected until airtight in the other. A remark is met at depth in one and audited for fairness in the other. Each account gives its own reason for the choice they make. The cost is that they rarely know which ruler has decided until the result is in their hands, and by then a settlement made under one account can read, under the other, as something they gave away.

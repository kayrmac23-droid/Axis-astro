## The Central Tension

The wide opposition between Mars and Jupiter is the one piece of linking evidence the plan supplies, and it crosses both frames without changing shape: Mars in the twelfth house holds something back from a room; Jupiter in the sixth wants to expand into exactly that room before the holding-back has finished its work. In the Tropical chart this is Libra Mars — detriment, deferring to consensus — against Aries Jupiter, peregrine and impatient to start the improved version now. The opposition there is a timing clash: Jupiter wants to move before Mars has decided what it is willing to risk saying. In the Sidereal chart the same angle runs through Virgo Mars, now peregrine but in Mercury's enemy sign, against Pisces Jupiter in its own domicile. Here the opposition is not about timing but about standard: Jupiter has already dissolved into the work, taking in the whole of a routine without needing a correct draft first, while Mars is still checking the draft against a precision Jupiter never asked for.

What refuses to agree is which planet is supposed to yield. The Tropical chart gives Jupiter the weaker dignity and Mars the compromised one, so neither side has standing to force the question, and the opposition simply recurs. The Sidereal chart hands Jupiter full domicile strength against a Mars working in territory that is not its own — an asymmetry the Tropical chart does not have. The ascendant lords carry this split into the body itself: Mars rules the Sidereal Lagna from the twelfth house, so the very instinct opening a room in that frame is the one habitually overruled by a domiciled Jupiter sitting in the sixth, confident of its own expansion and unconcerned with whether the twelfth-house correction has finished. Neither chart resolves who gets to go first.
